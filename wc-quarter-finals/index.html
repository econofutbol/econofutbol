Quarter Finals: A Narrower Gap, Same Old Story?

Four ties are set for the 2026 World Cup's Quarter Finals — and for the first time this tournament, every value gap sits inside a tight 2.2x-3.4x band. Here's how that range has played out across 80 knockout matches since 2006.

The Round of 16 tested squad value against everything from a near coin-flip 1.1x gap to a nearly 10x blowout. The Quarter Finals settle into a tighter, more predictable band: every remaining tie sits between 2.2x and 3.4x. We compare each matchup's price tag position by position, then check the data: across 80 knockout matches since 2006, how often has a gap this size actually held?

Match-by-Match Breakdowns

For each tie: the squad-value face-off, the top-10 most valuable players on each side, and how similar value gaps have played out across 80 historical knockout matches.

Morocco vs France: the Widest Gap of the Round

France's attack alone (€840m) is worth almost twice Morocco's entire 26-man squad (€447.7m) — the widest gap of any Quarter Final tie at 3.4x. Kylian Mbappé's price tag (€180m) more than doubles Morocco's most valuable player, Achraf Hakimi (€80m).

Mbappé's Output Still Dwarfs the Board

Kylian Mbappé (€180m) leads the matchup with 8 goal contributions (6 goals, 2 assists) through the Round of 16 — more than Morocco's entire top 10 combined. Ismael Saibari (€40m) tops Morocco's response with 3, and Achraf Hakimi adds a goal and an assist of his own.

At 3.4x, History Is Mostly — But Not Always — Kind to the Favorite

The closest historical comps to Morocco-France's 3.4x gap split 2 upsets in 5 — both decided on penalties. One of them is Morocco's own doing: their real 2022 shootout win over Spain, at a near-identical 3.22x gap. The other belongs to Croatia, who beat a favored Brazil the same way in the 2022 quarter-finals. At this range, the wallet still leads more often than not — but Morocco has already proven it can be the exception.

Belgium vs Spain: Spain's Attack Nearly Matches Belgium's Whole Squad

Spain's forward line alone (€477.8m) comes within €70m of matching Belgium's entire squad value (€547.5m) — part of a 2.23x gap overall. Lamine Yamal's €200m valuation is more than double Belgium's priciest player, Jérémy Doku (€75m).

The Real Top Scorers Are Missing From the Chart

Neither team's leading scorer cracks their own value chart: Spain's Mikel Oyarzabal (€25m) has 5 goal contributions, and Belgium's Romelu Lukaku (€6m) has 3 — both outproducing every player shown here. Among those who do make the cut, Youri Tielemans (2) and Spain's Álex Baena and Marc Cucurella (2 apiece) lead instead.

At 2.23x, the Favorite Has Never Lost

The 5 closest historical matches to Belgium-Spain's 2.23x gap are almost all Netherlands wins — over Argentina, Mexico, the USA and Uruguay — alongside Spain's own 2010 final win over the Netherlands. Zero upsets in five tries.

England vs Norway: the Priciest Player Wears the Cheaper Shirt

England's squad (€1,358m) outvalues Norway's (€589.9m) by 2.31x overall, and England's front line alone (€655m) costs more than Norway's entire squad. But the tournament's most expensive player isn't English: Erling Haaland (€200m) outprices Jude Bellingham (€130m) by €70m.

Kane Matches Haaland — Off His Own Team's Value Chart

Erling Haaland (€200m) leads the matchup outright with 5 goal contributions, but Harry Kane matches that tally exactly at just €60m — too cheap to crack England's own top 10. Among those shown, Jude Bellingham (€130m) leads England with 3.

At 2.31x, Nobody Has Ever Been Upset

Barely wider than Belgium-Spain's gap, England-Norway's 2.31x sits in the same safe territory: the same Netherlands-heavy string of results, plus Uruguay's 2010 win over South Korea. All five favorites won — none of them needed penalties to do it.

Argentina vs Switzerland: Double the Price, Almost Everywhere

Argentina's squad (€807.5m) is worth 2.43x Switzerland's €332.5m, and the gap is widest up front — Argentina's attack (€390m) costs more than 3.5 times Switzerland's (€106.8m). Julián Álvarez's €100m valuation is exactly double Switzerland's priciest player, Johan Manzambi (€50m) — the one metric where the two sides stay proportional.

Manzambi Outproduces Everyone — Including Messi's Teammates

Lionel Messi still has more goal contributions than anyone in this matchup — 7 — but at just €15m he doesn't crack Argentina's top 10 by value, same as every round before this one. That leaves Switzerland's Johan Manzambi (€50m) as the matchup's top producer on the chart, with 5 of his own; Argentina's Lisandro Martínez (€40m) leads their shown list with 2.

At 2.43x, the Favorite Wins — Sometimes Only on Penalties

Argentina-Switzerland's 2.43x gap draws comps split between comfortable wins (Netherlands over the USA and Uruguay) and two shootouts — Croatia over Japan in 2022 and England over Colombia in 2018. All five favorites won, but two of them needed penalties to prove it.

All four Quarter Final ties sit inside a tight 2.2x-3.4x band — a narrower, more consistent spread than the Round of 16's swing from a 1.1x coin flip to a nearly 10x blowout. History says the favorite wins comfortably at this range: three of four ties have never produced an upset at their exact gap.

The fourth — Morocco-France — is the one match where the underdog has already rewritten the script once this tournament, against a different opponent, at almost identical odds. Money still predicts the winner far more often than it should. Whether Morocco can make it happen twice is another question entirely.
