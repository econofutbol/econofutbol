From 5000-1 Champions to League One

How Leicester City built a dynasty, sold it piece by piece, and couldn't stop the fall.

On May 2nd, 2016, Leicester City completed one of the most improbable achievements in sporting history — winning the Premier League as 5000-1 outsiders. Less than ten years later, they have been relegated to League One — England's third division. This is the data story of how a champion collapses.

Built to Win, Built Differently

At the start of the 2015–16 season — the year Leicester won the Premier League — their squad was worth €161M, about half the league average of €210M.

They didn't buy their way to the title. Vardy came for free. Kanté cost €9M. Mahrez cost €500K.

But the squad that shocked the world was the squad every club wanted to buy — and Leicester couldn't say no forever.

The Quality Drain

They couldn't say no forever — and from 2017, the cost became visible. Departures consistently outweighed arrivals in quality. Leicester was exporting more value than it imported every single window.

The squad ceiling kept dropping, one sale at a time.

Spending More, Getting Less

Here's the twist: Leicester were net buyers in most windows — they spent more in fees than they received.

They paid premium fees for replacements. They just couldn't find another Kanté for €9M or a Mahrez for €500K.

The money was spent. The quality wasn't there.

Stripped for Parts

What happened to the squad that shocked the world

The 2016 title made Leicester's players the most wanted in Europe. Over the next nine years, the club sold over €1 billion in market value — champions shipped to direct rivals, fees spent on replacements that never matched the quality lost. Every window left the squad a little weaker.

Two Seasons, One Verdict

Compare the title season (2015–16) to the current Championship campaign (2025–26).

Squad market value: €161M → €145M. Summer arrivals MV: €41M → €46M. Summer departures MV: €34M → €156M.

The 2025 summer saw €156M of talent leave after relegation — replaced with €46M worth of new faces.

Sold to the Rivals

It wasn't just that the talent left. It's where it landed.

Maguire → Manchester United. Mahrez → Manchester City. Chilwell & Fofana → Chelsea. Maddison → Tottenham.

Nearly €300M in talent left for direct Top-6 competitors — strengthening the very clubs Leicester were trying to compete against.

The Asset Strip

Since the title win, Leicester exported over €1 billion in player market value.

Chelsea alone absorbed €139M worth of Leicester talent. Add Tottenham, City, and United and the Top 6 took 28% of every elite asset that left.

Those were not just transfers. They were individual careers — players who flourished at Leicester, then flourished elsewhere. The billions had faces.

The Human Cost

Player by player — who left, who replaced them

Behind the transfer fees and market values are individual careers. These are the players who passed through Leicester — some grew into world-class stars under the lights of the Premier League title run, others arrived as replacements and left quietly worth less than they came. The difference between those two groups tells the real story.

Value Created, Value Destroyed

Maguire signed for €8M and left for €50M. Maddison cost €10M and walked out worth €60M.

Kanté — perhaps the greatest bargain in football history — arrived for €4.5M and left worth €20M within a single season.

Leicester had an extraordinary eye for talent. The problem was they kept having to sell it.

The Rebuilds That Weren't

The replacements told the opposite story. Bertrand: €8M in, €0.8M out. Evans: €15M in, €2M out. Praet: €24M in, €4M out.

The scouting model that found Vardy and Kanté for pennies was gone. In its place: market-rate fees for players who declined at Leicester rather than flourished.

The ◆ diamond marks players who left out of contract — 16 of the 38 shown. Tielemans walked out worth €25M, Söyüncü at €15M, Iheanacho at €12M. Value that left for free.

Leicester's fall is not a story of bad luck. It's a story of a scouting model that couldn't be repeated — and a transfer strategy that spent heavily on players who couldn't fill the gaps left by champions.

The title was built on exceptional value identification: Vardy on a free, Kanté for €9M, Mahrez for €500K. Once those players left, Leicester paid market rates for less — and got exactly what they paid for.

What remains is a cautionary tale about sustaining success. Finding lightning once is genius. Bottling it is the hard part. From 5000-1 champions to fighting League One in nine years. Football is unforgiving.
