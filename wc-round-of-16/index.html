Round of 16: Money vs Matchups

All eight ties are set for the 2026 World Cup's Round of 16. Here's how the squad-value gap in each one compares to 80 knockout matches since 2006.

Squad market value has always been a rough proxy for World Cup favorites — but how rough? With the Round of 16 draw complete, we compare each matchup's price tag position by position, then check the data: across 80 knockout matches since 2006, how often has the value gap actually predicted the winner?

For each tie: the squad-value face-off, the top-10 most valuable players on each side, and how similar value gaps have played out across 80 historical knockout matches.

Canada vs Morocco: More Than Double the Price

First up, Morocco's squad is valued at €448m — more than double Canada's €199m. Achraf Hakimi alone (€80m) costs twice as much as Canada's most expensive player, Alphonso Davies (€40m), and Morocco's defence (€161m) outprices Canada's entire attack and midfield combined.

Jonathan David and Ismael Saibari Share the Lead

Despite that price gap, Canada's most expensive player is Alphonso Davies (€40m), but it's Jonathan David — valued at just €30m — who shares the lead in this matchup with 3 goal contributions so far this tournament. Morocco's Ismael Saibari (€40m) matches that tally exactly; Achraf Hakimi and Brahim Díaz follow with 2 apiece.

A 2.25x Gap Has Always Held

Individual battles aside, the 5 closest historical matches to Canada-Morocco's 2.25x value gap are mostly Netherlands ties from 2010-2022. In every single one, the pricier side won — Netherlands over Uruguay, Mexico and the USA, plus Argentina and Spain doing the same elsewhere. Zero upsets in five tries.

France vs Paraguay: the Biggest Mismatch of the Round

Canada-Morocco's 2.25x gap already looked wide. This one makes it look tame: France's squad is worth €1,523m — nearly 10 times Paraguay's €154m, the widest gap of any Round of 16 tie. Kylian Mbappé's market value alone (€180m) is bigger than Paraguay's entire squad (€154m).

Mbappé's Output Dwarfs Everyone Else's

And his output dwarfs the competition: Kylian Mbappé (€180m) has 8 goal contributions (6 goals, 2 assists) so far this tournament — more than Paraguay's entire top 10 combined. Paraguay's Julio Enciso leads their response with 3 of his own, at seven times cheaper.

At Nearly 10x, Nobody Has Ever Been Upset

Zoom out to the history books and the pattern repeats: France-Paraguay's 9.9x gap sits among the most lopsided in Round of 16 history. The closest comps — Italy over Australia, Germany over Algeria, Belgium over Japan — all went the way of the bigger wallet. Costa Rica came closest to rewriting that history, taking a heavily favored Netherlands (8.7x) through 120 scoreless minutes in the 2014 quarter-finals before the bigger wallet finally came through on penalties. No upsets from this far back in 80 knockout matches since 2006.

Brazil vs Norway: Closer Than the Names Suggest

Not every tie in this round is this lopsided, though. Brazil's €928m squad still leads Norway's €590m, but at 1.57x this is the tightest value gap of the round so far. Erling Haaland (€200m) is worth more than any Brazilian, including Vinícius Júnior (€140m) — the "smaller" squad owns the most valuable player on the pitch.

Vinícius Júnior and Haaland Are Tied at the Top

That price tag hasn't been the difference-maker either: Brazil's Vinícius Júnior (€140m) and Norway's Erling Haaland (€200m) are tied at 5 goal contributions each, despite the €60m gap between them. Bruno Guimarães isn't far behind with 4, all assists, for Brazil.

This Is Where Upsets Actually Happen

That closeness matters historically, too. Brazil-Norway's 1.57x gap lands in genuinely dangerous territory: 2 of the 5 closest historical comps ended in upsets — Uruguay over Portugal in 2018, and Portugal over England on penalties after 120 minutes locked at 0-0 in 2006. When the value gap shrinks this far, the favorite stops being a safe bet.

Brazil Keeps Showing Up at This Exact Ratio — and Keeps Winning

Portugal vs Spain: the Tightest Gap of the Round So Far

Even tighter than Brazil-Norway's 1.57x: Spain's squad (€1,222.8m) edges Portugal's (€1,005.5m) by just 1.22x — the closest gap of any tie so far. Lamine Yamal's €200m valuation ties him as the tournament's priciest player, while Portugal counters with João Neves (€140m) anchoring a defence (€374.5m) that trails Spain's (€385m) by less than €11m.

The Real Goal Leaders Aren't Anywhere Near the Value Chart

Cristiano Ronaldo (€10m) and Mikel Oyarzabal (€25m) have more goal contributions than anyone shown here — 3 and 5 — but neither cracks their team's top 10 by value. Among those who do, Portugal's Rafael Leão (€50m) and Spain's Marc Cucurella and Álex Baena (€50m and €40m) lead with 2 apiece.

At 1.22x, the Underdog Has Won Once in Five Tries

That tightness matters historically, too: of the 5 closest matches to Portugal-Spain's 1.22x gap, only one has been an upset — Germany over Argentina on penalties after 120 minutes locked at 1-1 in the 2006 quarter-finals. The other four went to the pricier side, Portugal included: they beat the Netherlands at this exact range back in 2006.

Switzerland vs Colombia: the Tightest Gap of the Round

Even tighter than Portugal-Spain's 1.22x: Switzerland's squad (€332.5m) edges Colombia's (€302.35m) by just 1.10x — the closest gap of any Round of 16 tie. Luis Díaz's €70m valuation is Colombia's biggest, while Switzerland counters with Johan Manzambi (€50m) anchoring a midfield (€122.5m) worth more than double Colombia's (€51m).

Manzambi Outproduces Everyone in This Tie

Switzerland's Johan Manzambi (€50m) leads the matchup with 5 goal contributions — more than any other player shown. Colombia's response is split between two players at 2 apiece: Luis Díaz (€70m) and Daniel Muñoz (€22m).

At 1.1x, It's Basically a Coin Flip

History says this one is the closest thing to a toss-up in the whole round: of the 5 closest matches to Switzerland-Colombia's 1.10x gap, 2 have been upsets — Sweden over a favored Switzerland in 2018, and France over England in the 2022 quarter-finals. Two more never even settled it in 120 minutes: Ukraine-Switzerland (2006) finished level at 0-0, and Argentina-Netherlands (2022 quarter-finals) finished level at 2-2 — both went to penalties. At the tightest gaps in Round of 16 history, the favorite's edge all but disappears.

All eight Round of 16 matches are now locked in, and the market-value story splits in two: blowout gaps like France-Paraguay, England-Mexico and Argentina-Egypt have never produced an upset in 80 knockout matches since 2006, while tighter ties like Brazil-Norway, Portugal-Spain, USA-Belgium and Switzerland-Colombia sit in genuinely competitive territory.

With the full bracket set, the pattern holds: money predicts the winner far more often than it should — but the tightest gaps are exactly where it stops being a safe bet.
