Semifinals: One Safe Bet, One Coin Flip

Both Semifinal ties are set — and their squad-value gaps couldn't tell more different stories. France-Spain sits at a relatively contained 1.25x, where the wallet has won 24 of 33 comparable knockout matches since 2006. England-Argentina's 1.69x gap is close to a coin flip: 13 favorites, 11 upsets — and England already knows what losing from here feels like.

Both Semifinal matchups are locked in, and their squad-value gaps sit worlds apart. France-Spain's 1.25x gap is the tighter of the two — and history says favorites at this range usually win, if rarely by much. England-Argentina's 1.69x gap is a different story: the closest historical comps split almost down the middle, and England has been on the losing end of a gap this size twice before. We compare each side's price tag position by position, then check 108 knockout matches since 2006 — this tournament's own included — to see what each gap has actually meant.

For each tie: the squad-value face-off, the top-10 most valuable players on each side, the closest historical matches where the favorite held and where it didn't, and a wider map of every comparable result since 2006.

France vs Spain: A Deeper Squad, But Not Everywhere

France's squad (€1,523m) outvalues Spain's (€1,222.8m) overall, and the gap is widest up front — France's attack (€840m) costs 1.76x Spain's (€477.8m). Flip to midfield and the gap disappears entirely: Spain's engine room (€360m) actually outvalues France's (€222m), the only metric where the underdog leads. Lamine Yamal's €200m valuation still tops Kylian Mbappé's €180m by €20m — the priciest player in this matchup wears red, not blue.

Mbappé's Output Dwarfs Spain's Entire Response

Kylian Mbappé (€180m) leads the matchup with 11 combined goal contributions (8 goals, 3 assists) — more than Spain's entire top 10 combined. Lamine Yamal, at €200m the priciest player on the board, has managed just 1. Ousmane Dembélé (€100m, 7 G+A) is the next-most productive; Marc Cucurella and Álex Baena lead Spain's response with 2 apiece.

At 1.25x, Favorites Win — But Rarely Comfortably

The closest historical matches to this 1.25x gap that went as expected were tight almost without exception — four of five finished 1-0. Portugal supplies two of them: beaten by Spain in this tournament's own Round of 16 (1.22x) and by France in the 2006 Semifinal (1.24x), a near-exact repeat of tonight's gap. At this range, the price tag usually wins — but rarely with room to spare.

At 1.25x, the Underdog Has a Blueprint — and France Wrote Half of It

Two of the five closest upsets at this gap belong to France itself — beating Brazil 1-0 in the 2006 Quarter Final and England 2-1 in 2022, both times as the cheaper side. Germany supplies the other headline upsets: a penalty shootout over Argentina in 2006, and a 4-1 demolition of England in 2010. If this gap ever turns, both teams tonight already know what it looks like — France has been the one springing it.

Zoom Out: 33 Matches at This Range, Nine Went the Other Way

Widen the lens to every knockout match within 0.3x of this 1.25x gap — 33 in total, spanning 2006 to this tournament's own Round of 32 — and the picture holds: favorites won 24 of them. The nine upsets, France's pair among them, sit scattered across two decades rather than clustered in any one era. History doesn't rule out a repeat tonight, but it's the exception, not the rule.

England vs Argentina: Even at Midfield, a Gulf at the Back

England's squad (€1,358m) outvalues Argentina's (€807.5m) by a wide margin, but the gap isn't even across the pitch. Midfield is almost dead level — Argentina's engine room (€244m) sits within €22m of England's (€266.2m), the tightest metric in the matchup. Defence tells the opposite story: England's back line (€437m) costs 2.5x Argentina's (€173.5m), the widest spread of the five. Jude Bellingham's €130m valuation leads the matchup outright, €30m clear of Argentina's priciest player, Julián Alvarez (€100m).

Kane Matches Bellingham — at Half the Price and Off the Chart

Jude Bellingham (€130m) leads the shown chart with 7 combined goal contributions (6 goals, 1 assist), but Harry Kane matches that exact tally at just €60m — cheap enough to miss England's own top 10 by a single spot. Lionel Messi's story repeats yet again: still the matchup's most productive player outright, but at €15m he doesn't crack Argentina's value chart either. Among those who do, Lautaro Martínez (€85m, 3 G+A) leads Argentina's response.

At 1.69x, Argentina's Already Been Here — and Lost

Argentina has stood on this side of almost this exact gap before: as the €900m underdog against France's €1,616m in the 2018 Round of 16, and lost 4-3, the highest-scoring match in this entire comparison set. The other four comps split between routine 1-0 wins and one shootout — Croatia needed penalties to see off Russia in 2018 despite being favored. At this range, the price tag usually holds, but not always without drama.

At 1.69x, England's Already Lost a Semifinal Here

England has been upset from almost this exact gap twice — on penalties against Portugal in the 2006 Quarter Final, and outright, 2-1, against Croatia in the 2018 Semifinal, the same round England plays tonight. This tournament has already added two more reminders that the range is live: Norway over Brazil in the Round of 16, and Morocco's shootout win over the Netherlands in the Round of 32.

Zoom Out: Nearly a Coin Flip

Widen the lens to every knockout match within 0.3x of this 1.69x gap — 24 in total — and the near-even split holds up: 13 favorites, 11 upsets. That's the less predictable of the two Semifinal gaps by a wide margin, and it lines up with everything else this section has shown: at this range, England's own history is more cautionary tale than comfort blanket.

Two ties, two very different odds. France-Spain's 1.25x gap has favored the wallet 24 times out of 33 in comparable knockout matches since 2006 — and on the nine occasions it hasn't, France itself has often been the one making history repeat. England-Argentina's 1.69x gap is close to a coin flip, 13-11, and England already knows what it's like to lose from here: on penalties in 2006, and in a Semifinal outright in 2018.

Money still predicts the winner more often than not at both ranges — just not by nearly as much on one side of this draw as the other.
